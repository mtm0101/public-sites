Q: How do people usually get local news nowadays?
Most people get local news through their phones — Facebook, news apps, online portals, and
increasingly TikTok. Traditional outlets like TV and newspapers are losing ground, especially
among younger audiences. There's also a rise of community group chats on Zalo or Facebook,
where neighbors share information about local events, scams, or traffic. It's faster and more
personal than mainstream media.
Q: Do you think local news is more important than international news? Why?
It depends on context. Local news is more immediately relevant — it affects your daily commute,
weather, neighborhood events, or local policies. However, international news shapes the broader

world we live in, including the economy and global trends. Ideally, people should follow both, but
they often have different priorities at different stages of life.
Q: Why are some news stories more popular than others?
Popular stories usually tap into strong emotions — outrage, sympathy, curiosity, or humor. Stories
involving celebrities, scandals, or dramatic events get clicks. Algorithms on social media also
amplify content that gets engagement, creating a feedback loop. Unfortunately, this means serious
but "boring" news often gets overlooked while sensational stories dominate the feed.
Q: Do social media affect the way people receive news?
Hugely. Social media has democratized news consumption — anyone can be a publisher now.
The downside is that misinformation spreads just as fast as facts, and people often get trapped in
filter bubbles that confirm their existing views. People also tend to scroll headlines rather than
read full articles, leading to a more superficial understanding of issues.

Q: What kinds of technological problems do people often face?
The most common ones are slow internet, software crashes, and forgotten passwords. Beyond
that, people deal with phones running out of battery at critical moments, app updates that break
features they relied on, and compatibility issues between devices. For older users, just navigating
new interfaces and confusing menus can be a major hurdle.
Q: How has technology made people's lives easier?
Tremendously. We can communicate instantly across the globe, access vast amounts of
information at our fingertips, and automate tedious tasks like calculations, shopping, or
remembering appointments. Healthcare, education, transport — all have been revolutionized. Even
small things like GPS navigation save hours that previous generations would have spent getting
lost.
Q: Do you think people depend too much on technology nowadays?
Yes, definitely. Many people can't go ten minutes without checking their phone, and basic skills
like map-reading or mental math are fading. When the internet goes down, productivity collapses.
While technology brings incredible benefits, this over-reliance can be risky — both for
individuals and for society as a whole.
Q: What should people do when technology fails?
Stay calm and have fallback options. Keeping a paper notebook for important contacts, knowing
basic directions without GPS, and saving offline copies of critical documents all help. It's also
smart to know basic troubleshooting — restarting devices, checking connections, contacting
support. Above all, being patient is key; tech problems are often temporary inconveniences rather
than disasters.
Q: Will technology become more reliable in the future?

I'm cautiously optimistic. AI and machine learning should help systems self-diagnose and fix
problems automatically. Hardware is getting more durable, and software is becoming more userfriendly. However, as systems get more complex, they also create new failure points. So while
individual issues may decrease, the interconnected nature of modern tech means that when
something fails, it can affect many things at once.

Q: Why do some people prefer waking up early?
Early risers love the peaceful atmosphere before the world wakes up — no notifications, no
traffic, just quiet productivity. Many highly successful people swear by morning routines because
it gives them a head start on the day. There's also a psychological boost from accomplishing things
before most people even get out of bed; it feels like a win to start the day.
Q: Do young people and older people have different sleeping habits?

Absolutely. Young people, especially teenagers and university students, tend to be night owls —
staying up late and sleeping in. Their biological clocks are naturally shifted later. Older people,
by contrast, often wake up early without an alarm and go to bed sooner. This isn't just preference;
it's partly due to changes in circadian rhythms as we age.
Q: Is it healthier to wake up early? Why?
Generally, yes — but it depends on whether you're getting enough sleep overall. Waking up early
aligns better with natural daylight, which supports mental clarity and mood. It also makes time
for healthy habits like breakfast and exercise. However, forcing yourself to wake up early while
sleeping only 5 hours is far worse than sleeping in and getting 8.
Q: What jobs require people to wake up very early?
Many jobs do. Bakers and chefs often start before dawn to prepare food for the day. Farmers wake
up early to tend to crops and animals. Pilots, flight attendants, doctors on morning shifts, and TV
news anchors all have early starts. Office cleaners and delivery drivers also begin early to finish
before peak hours.

1. What kinds of ambitions do people have?
Different people have different ambitions. But the majority of people want to have a stable source
of income to maintain an acceptable living standard. Some others who are even more ambitious
will strive themselves to make their way to the top.
2. Why should parents encourage children to have ambitions?
Encouraging children to have ambitions allows them to know what they’re doing so that they can
identify their dreams and follow the right way to achieve them. Research suggests that encouraging
children to develop an ambitious mindset is strongly associated with successful children, as those
raised in this way are more likely to thrive academically, socially, and are less likely to take
unhealthy risks as teens.
3. Should parents interfere with children’s ambitions?
This depends. If children are down to earth and have practical ambitions, parents should not
interfere with their ambitions. By contrast, if children overestimate their capabilities and have
seemingly impossible ambitions, it is imperative that parents give their children valuable pieces of
advice so that they can have more practical ambitions.
4. Is there any difference between children’s ambitions and those of grown-ups?
Absolutely yes. There are many differences between children’s ambitions and those of grown-ups
indeed. While children simply want to have beautiful clothes, academic brilliance and excellent
academic results, adults seek wealth while hoping they will also have time to recharge their
batteries by going on luxurious trips.

Q: Why do people sometimes ignore messages?
There are many reasons. Some people are simply overwhelmed with work or personal issues and
lose the mental energy to reply. Others have what's called message anxiety — they see the
message, plan to respond thoughtfully later, then forget entirely. And let's be honest, occasionally
people ignore messages on purpose because they're avoiding a difficult conversation or aren't
interested anymore.
Q: Is online communication better than face-to-face communication?
Each has its strengths. Online communication is more convenient — you can talk to anyone
anywhere, anytime, which is great for staying in touch with distant friends. However, face-to-face
is far richer because you can read body language, tone, and facial expressions. For deep or
emotional conversations, nothing beats meeting in person. The best approach is to use both
depending on the situation.
Q: Do young people communicate differently from older people?
Definitely. Younger people rely heavily on text-based and visual communication — emojis,
memes, voice notes, short videos. They prefer asynchronous chatting where they can reply when
convenient. Older generations often favor phone calls or longer, more formal messages. This gap

can sometimes cause misunderstandings, but it also creates interesting cross-generational
learning opportunities.
Q: How has technology changed communication?
Technology has made communication instantaneous and borderless. We can video call relatives
across the globe, send photos in seconds, and access information at our fingertips. On the flip side,
it's made us more impatient — we expect immediate replies. It's also created an illusion of
connection while sometimes reducing the depth of real human interaction.
Q: Is it rude not to reply to messages? Why?
It depends on context. Failing to reply to important or personal messages is generally considered

unnecessary conflict. Excessive texting can also replace meaningful in-person time, weakening
bonds. On top of that, the "left on read" phenomenon — when someone reads your message but
doesn't reply — can cause real anxiety and resentment in close relationships.

Q: Why do companies use celebrities in advertisements?
Companies use celebrities mainly because they bring instant brand recognition and emotional
appeal. When a famous person endorses a product, fans often feel a personal connection and are
more willing to trust the brand. Celebrities also help products stand out in a crowded market, and
their image — whether glamorous, athletic, or trustworthy — gets transferred onto the product.
Q: Do celebrity advertisements influence consumers?
Yes, quite significantly, especially among young people. Many consumers, consciously or
unconsciously, want to emulate their favorite stars by buying what they use. However, the
influence isn't unlimited. Smart consumers can tell the difference between a genuine endorsement
and a paid promotion, and badly chosen celebrities can actually backfire on a brand.

Q: Are advertisements more creative nowadays?
Definitely. With so much competition for attention, brands have had to up their game. Today's ads
often tell short cinematic stories, use stunning visuals, or play on emotions. Social media has also
forced advertisers to be more interactive and viral-friendly. Compared to the simple productfocused ads of decades past, modern advertising is more sophisticated.
Q: What makes an advertisement effective?
An effective ad usually does three things: grabs attention quickly, communicates a clear message,
and creates an emotional connection. Memorable visuals, catchy slogans, and a sense of
authenticity all help. Most importantly, the ad needs to be relevant to its target audience — even
the most beautiful commercial fails if it speaks to the wrong people.
Q: Do children get influenced by advertisements easily?
Yes, far more easily than adults. Children lack the critical thinking skills to recognize persuasion
techniques, so they tend to take what they see at face value. They also imitate what they see —
especially when their favorite cartoon or influencer is involved. This is why many countries have
strict rules limiting ads aimed at children, particularly for unhealthy products.
Q: Should there be stricter control of advertising?
Yes, in certain areas. Advertising of products that can harm health — like junk food, alcohol, or
tobacco — should be tightly regulated, especially when targeted at minors. Misleading claims also
deserve harsher penalties. However, over-regulation could stifle creativity and harm small
businesses, so the rules need to be balanced and well-targeted.

Q: Why do some people find it difficult to make important decisions?
Big decisions often come with high stakes and uncertain outcomes, which naturally creates
anxiety. Some people are perfectionists who fear making the wrong choice, while others struggle
with decision fatigue — they're overwhelmed by too many options. Lack of confidence, fear of
regret, and pressure from family or society can also paralyze a person when making important
calls.
Q: Do young people make decisions differently from older people?
Yes, quite differently. Young people tend to be more impulsive and willing to take risks — they
have less to lose and time on their side. Older people, by contrast, usually weigh things more
carefully, drawing on past experience and considering long-term consequences. Neither approach
is inherently better; they suit different stages of life.
Q: Who do people usually ask for advice when making important decisions?
Most commonly, people turn to those they trust deeply — family members, especially parents, and
close friends. For career-related decisions, mentors and senior colleagues are often consulted.
Some also seek objective opinions from professionals like financial advisors or counselors, who
can offer expertise without emotional bias.
Q: Do you think people should always think carefully before making decisions?
For major decisions, absolutely. Rushing into things can lead to regret. However, overthinking
small choices wastes mental energy. The trick is matching the level of deliberation to the

importance of the decision. For trivial matters, going with your gut is often fine; for life-changing
ones, careful analysis is essential.
Q: Can difficult decisions help people grow as individuals? Why?
Without a doubt. Difficult decisions force us to clarify our values, weigh trade-offs, and take
responsibility for our lives. They build resilience and self-awareness. Even when the outcome isn't
ideal, the process of making the decision teaches us about ourselves. People who avoid hard
choices often remain stuck in their comfort zone.
Q: Do you prefer making decisions alone or with other people's advice?
I prefer a balanced approach. For decisions that affect mainly me, I like to think things through
alone first, then seek advice from one or two trusted people. Their outside perspective often
catches blind spots. But ultimately, I believe the final call must be mine — only I have to live with
the consequences.
